# FEESBEES — Ad Copy Sheet

Paste-ready copy for all 10 creatives. Character counts are Meta's truncation
points, not hard limits: **primary text** truncates around 125 characters on
mobile, **headline** around 40, **description** around 30. The first line of
every primary text below is written to survive that truncation on its own.

Anything in `[BRACKETS]` is unconfirmed — see the warning at the end.

## Ad 01 — "The Uniform"
**Funnel:** TOF · **Format:** Hero brand statement · **Tests:** identity-led awareness

**Visual:** The black tee with the oversized tan bull lockup, worn, shot
three-quarter against a dark barn-wood wall. Single hard key light from camera
left, deep shadow on the right. The shirt is the only bright thing in frame.

**On-image text:**
> `NOT A TREND.`
> `A UNIFORM.`
> — `FEESBEES · EST. 2019`

**Primary text:**
> We only ever made one promise: a shirt you stop thinking about.
>
> Heavyweight cotton. A collar that holds its shape. A bull that doesn't need
> explaining. It goes on Tuesday morning and it goes on again Thursday, and
> five years in that's still the whole idea.
>
> FEESBEES. Built for the ones who show up.

**Headline:** `Not A Trend. A Uniform.`
**Description:** `Heavyweight tees · est. 2019`
**CTA:** Shop Now

**Why it works:** Cold audiences don't buy features, they buy a category they
already belong to. "Uniform" says *this is what people like you wear* without
claiming anything that needs a footnote.

## Ad 02 — "Five Washes"
**Funnel:** TOF · **Format:** Problem vs solution split · **Tests:** durability pain

**Visual:** Hard vertical split. **Left** — a generic grey tee on a hanger,
collar warped into a wave, surface pilled, dull flat light. **Right** — the
white FEESBEES badge tee, collar crisp, print sharp, clean light. Left side
desaturated and slightly cool; right side warm and true.

**On-image text:**
> Left panel: `THE $15 TEE` / `AFTER 5 WASHES`
> Right panel: `FEESBEES` / `AFTER 50`
> Bottom bar: `YOUR SHIRT SHOULDN'T RETIRE BEFORE THE SEASON DOES`

**Primary text:**
> You already know this shirt. Bought in March, collar's a noodle by June, rag
> by fall.
>
> That's not bad luck. That's thin fabric and a single-stitch hem doing exactly
> what they were built to do — get you back in the store.
>
> Ours is `[GSM]` combed cotton with a ribbed collar that snaps back and a
> double-needle hem that doesn't unravel. Buy it once.

**Headline:** `Cheap Tees Aren't Cheap`
**Description:** `Built to outlast the season`
**CTA:** Shop Now

**Why it works:** The problem is one the buyer has personally experienced and
can picture in his own drawer. Naming the mechanism — thin fabric, single
stitch — is what turns a complaint into a reason to believe.

## Ad 06 — "Est. 2019"
**Funnel:** TOF → MOF · **Format:** Founder / heritage · **Tests:** trust

**Visual:** The bull lockup treated as a stamped brand mark — burned into
leather or branded on wood — with the black big-logo tee below it. Warm, dim,
tactile. Heritage without pretending to be older than 2019.

**On-image text:**
> `STARTED WITH ONE SHIRT.`
> `STILL MAKING IT.`
> `EST. 2019`

**Primary text:**
> 2019. One shirt, one colour, a bull we drew because it looked like how the
> work felt.
>
> We've added colours since. We haven't changed the cut, the weight, or the
> collar, because the people who bought the first one are still wearing it and
> they'd tell us.
>
> That's the entire brand history. No heritage story, no fake founding date.

**Headline:** `One Shirt. Still Making It.`
**Description:** `FEESBEES · est. 2019`
**CTA:** Learn More

**Why it works:** Small brands lose to trust, not to price. Owning a recent
founding date is more persuasive than implying an old one, and it inoculates
against the "who even are you" objection before the MOF ads have to fight it.

## Ad 07 — "Anatomy"
**Funnel:** MOF · **Format:** Product demo, spec callouts · **Tests:** quality proof

**Visual:** The white badge tee laid flat, shot straight down, generous margin.
Thin callout rules run from six points on the garment to labels in the margin.
Technical-drawing feel — restrained, not busy.

**On-image callouts:**
1. `RIBBED CREW COLLAR` — recovers, doesn't wave
2. `[GSM] COMBED COTTON` — opaque in daylight
3. `LEFT-CHEST PRINT` — printed soft, not a stiff patch
4. `TONAL SLEEVE HIT` — the detail you notice second
5. `DOUBLE-NEEDLE HEM` — the seam that usually fails first
6. `RELAXED STRAIGHT BODY` — `[FIT]`, not tapered

**Header:** `EVERY DETAIL, ON PURPOSE`

**Primary text:**
> Six things we argued about longer than we should have.
>
> The collar is ribbed so it recovers instead of waving. The body is `[GSM]`,
> heavy enough that you can't see your hand through it. The chest hit is printed
> soft, so it moves with the shirt instead of sitting on top of it like a
> sticker. The hem is double-needle, because that's the seam that goes first.

**Headline:** `Every Detail, On Purpose`
**Description:** `[GSM] combed cotton`
**CTA:** Shop Now

**Why it works:** Warm traffic has already decided they like it and are now

## Ad 09 — "vs. The $15 Tee"
**Funnel:** MOF · **Format:** Comparison table · **Tests:** price objection

**Visual:** Two columns. Left header `FEESBEES` on the brand's dark field; right
header `TYPICAL $15 TEE` in flat grey. Six rows, ticks left, crosses right. The
sand tee sits small at the top as the anchor.

**On-image table:**

| | FEESBEES | Typical $15 tee |
|---|---|---|
| Fabric weight | `[GSM]` | ~140 gsm |
| Collar after 20 washes | Holds shape | Waves out |
| Hem | Double-needle | Single stitch |
| Chest logo | Printed soft | Stiff transfer, cracks |
| Fit after washing | True to size | Shrinks up, boxy |
| Replaced every | `[X] years` | ~1 season |

**Footer:** `CHEAP TEES AREN'T CHEAP — THEY'RE JUST CHEAP MORE OFTEN.`

**Primary text:**
> Four cheap tees a year, or one that's still in rotation in three.
>
> We're not going to pretend `[PRICE]` isn't more than a fast-fashion tee. It
> is. The question is what you're actually buying — and at `[GSM]` against
> roughly 140, you're buying about twice the shirt.
>
> Run the maths on a year of replacements and this stops being the expensive
> option.

**Headline:** `Cheap Tees Aren't Cheap`
**Description:** `Do the maths on a year`
**CTA:** Shop Now

**Why it works:** Reframes price as cost-per-year, which is the only frame in
which a premium basic wins. Conceding the price up front — *it is more* — buys
the credibility to make the comparison.

> **Before shipping this one:** the 140 gsm competitor figure is a category
> generalisation, not a measured claim about a named brand. Keep it generic.
> Never name a competitor, and don't run this creative at all until `[GSM]` is
> confirmed — the whole ad is one unverified number away from being false.

## ⚠️ Before any of this goes live

**1. Fill every bracket.** `[GSM]`, `[FABRIC]`, `[PRICE]`, `[BUNDLE_PRICE]`,
`[SIZES]`, `[FIT]`, `[RETURNS]`, `[SHIP_TIME]`, `[X] years`. The storefront was
unreachable from this environment, so not one of these is confirmed. Ads 02,
07 and 09 are built on the fabric weight and do not stand up without it.

**2. Reviews must be real.** Ads 08 and 10 are laid out for genuine customer
reviews and shipped with placeholder text on purpose. Invented testimonials
are false advertising, and the FTC's 2024 rule on fake and AI-generated reviews
attaches civil penalties per violation. If there's no review corpus yet, run
the other eight and hold these until there is.

**3. Keep the comparison generic.** Ad 09 compares against an unnamed category
average. Naming a competitor turns a puffery claim into one you have to be able
to substantiate in court.

**4. The models in these creatives are AI-generated.** They are not customers
and must never be captioned as such. If a rendered person is ever implied to be
a real buyer, that's the same violation as a fake review.
